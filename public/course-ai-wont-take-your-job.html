Intro · 10 Minutes

The Fear Is Legitimate — And Partially Wrong

"AI will eliminate 300 million jobs in the next decade."

These headlines aren't wrong. They're just incomplete. They tell you what disappears. They don't tell you what replaces it.

The threat isn't the technology. It's falling behind the people who adapt.

This Has Happened Before

TechnologyWhat "died"What actually happened
ATMs (1970s)Bank teller jobsTeller count went up — branches expanded, roles shifted
Spreadsheets (1980s)Bookkeeper jobsAccountants grew in number — more financial analysis, not less
Search engines (2000s)Research jobsMore research roles, not fewer — the bar for output rose

Tasks disappear. Jobs evolve. AI is following the same pattern — faster, broader, but the same shape.

Every Job Has Three Zones

Zone 1

AI Takes Over

Tasks AI does faster, cheaper, better. These disappear from your job description.

Formatting reports, scheduling, summarizing, first-draft writing
Zone 2

AI Amplifies You

Tasks where AI makes you 5–10x more productive. You still own the work.

Research, analysis, proposal writing, client prep, presentations
Zone 3

AI Can't Touch

Tasks that require judgment, trust, relationships, context, physical presence.

Reading a room, building client relationships, managing through change

Every job — yours included — has all three zones. The ratio is what matters.

Module 1 · 15 Minutes

What AI Actually Does

Plain English. No jargon.

What AI Actually Is

  • AI is a pattern-matching machine trained on more text than any human could read in a thousand lifetimes
  • It doesn't think. It predicts what comes next based on patterns it's seen.
  • It's extraordinarily good at tasks that have patterns: writing, summarizing, classifying, translating, answering questions
  • It has no judgment, no lived experience, no skin in the game
  • When it sounds confident and is wrong, it's not lying — it's pattern-matching poorly

Understanding this one thing changes how you use AI at work.

The Only Split That Matters

Pattern TasksJudgment Tasks
Has a clear right answerRequires weighing competing values
Can be learned from examplesRequires context AI doesn't have
Improves with repetitionRequires accountability
Low cost to be wrongHigh cost to be wrong
AI does this wellAI does this poorly

HR: Scheduling interviews = pattern. Deciding who to hire = judgment.
Legal: Summarizing a contract = pattern. Advising on risk = judgment.

What AI Can't Do (Yet)

  • Build trust — clients, patients, customers trust people, not systems
  • Be accountable — when something goes wrong, someone has to own it
  • Read a room — emotional intelligence, body language, what's not being said
  • Handle novelty well — AI fails when a situation has no pattern to match
  • Know what it doesn't know — it confidently fills gaps with plausible-sounding wrong answers

The roles that survive and grow are the ones where the cost of being wrong is high, and the value of human judgment is visible.

Module 2 · 20 Minutes · The Task Audit

Your Job Through an AI Lens

Stop Thinking About Your Job Title. Think About Your Tasks.

  • Job titles are lagging indicators — they describe what you did, not what you do
  • AI doesn't eliminate job titles. It eliminates tasks within jobs.
  • The PM who understands AI keeps their job and gets promoted. The PM who doesn't gets replaced by the one who does.
  • The only way to know your real exposure is to audit your actual tasks — not your job description

Your job title is not your job. Your tasks are.

The Task Audit: Three Categories

AI-Vulnerable

AI does it better

Tasks AI can already do, or will within 2 years. AI does it faster, cheaper, at scale.

Your goal: stop spending time here.
AI-Enhanced

AI makes you 5–10×

You still own the output. AI does the heavy lifting.

Your goal: become the expert at using AI here.
AI-Proof

Your career moat

Tasks requiring judgment, trust, relationships, accountability. The value of doing these goes up as AI handles everything else.

Your goal: go deeper here.

A Real Task Audit: HR Manager

TaskCategoryWhat changes
Scheduling interviewsAI-VulnerableAI handles this now
Writing job descriptionsAI-VulnerableAI generates drafts in seconds
Screening resumes for fitAI-EnhancedAI flags patterns; you decide
Designing comp structuresAI-EnhancedAI models scenarios; you recommend
Managing a difficult performance conversationAI-ProofTrust, judgment, legal nuance
Building relationships with hiring managersAI-ProofInfluence, context, history

This HR manager's job doesn't disappear. The first two rows disappear from her job. She gains capacity to go deeper on the last three.

What Your Ratio Tells You

Mostly AI-Vulnerable

High exposure

Most time on tasks AI does better. Not a crisis — a signal to upskill and shift toward enhanced + proof tasks. The shift can happen in your current role.

Mostly AI-Enhanced

Strong position

You're in the leverage zone. AI makes you more valuable, not less. Your job: learn to use these tools well before someone else does.

Mostly AI-Proof

Protected

Your work depends on judgment, relationships, accountability. These roles grow in value. Your risk is underestimating how AI can amplify you.

No ratio is fatal. Every ratio is actionable.

Module 3 · 20 Minutes

The Five-Hour Plan

You don't need to become an AI expert. You need to be the person at work who understands AI.

The Wrong Goal and the Right One

The wrong goal

"I need to learn AI."

Too broad. No finish line. Paralyzing. Most "learn AI" content is either too technical or too surface-level to be useful at work.

The right goal

"I need to be the most AI-literate person in my immediate team."

Finite. Achievable. In most teams, that bar is lower than you think. The person who clears it first gets the interesting projects.

You don't need to beat AI. You need to be ahead of the colleagues who are ignoring it.

Five Hours. Starting Monday.

1
Understand what AI actually isRead one good explainer. Goal: explain pattern vs. judgment to a colleague in 2 minutes.
2
Run your Task AuditDo the worksheet from Module 2. Share it with one person you trust.
3
Use AI for one real task at workPick a task from your AI-Enhanced list. Do it with AI assistance. Note what surprised you.
4
Find the AI tool your industry usesLegal has Harvey. HR has Lattice AI. Finance has Planful. Know what your peers are already using.
5
Write one paragraph about AI's impact on your rolePlain language. No jargon. When someone asks what you think about AI at work, you have an answer.

Why Five Hours Is Enough to Get Ahead

The average professional has spent 0 structured hours thinking about AI's impact on their specific role. Most "AI anxiety" comes from consuming headlines, not doing experiments. Five structured hours puts you ahead of 80% of your colleagues — not because you know more, but because you've actually engaged.

The gap isn't about technical knowledge. It's about willingness to engage before it's urgent.

Close · 10 Minutes

The Mindset Shift

From "AI vs. Me" to "AI With Me"

The anxiety frame
  • AI is coming for my job.
  • I need to defend my territory.
  • If AI can do my job, I'm replaceable.
The leverage frame
  • AI handles the tasks I find tedious.
  • I can do more valuable work with the same hours.
  • My judgment, relationships, and accountability are worth more — not less.

The professionals who lose out aren't the ones AI replaces. They're the ones who spent the next three years waiting to see what happens.

  • Tell one person what you learned today — the act of explaining it locks it in

The information without the action is just more content. These three things change that.
