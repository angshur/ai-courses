Intro · 5 Minutes

The Question Everyone Gets Wrong

"Which model should we use?"

Teams spend weeks evaluating models. Months debating fine-tuning vs. RAG. And then a competitor ships a better product with the same model they rejected last quarter. The model is not the moat. It never was.

If your competitive advantage lives in which model you chose, your competitor's next API call erases it.

Module 1 · 8 Minutes

Why Context Is Now the Moat

How the Moat Moved

Then (2022–2023)Now (2025+)
Model access was the moatEvery team has access to the same frontier models
GPT-4 was a competitive edgeClaude, GPT-4o, Gemini — all commodity APIs
Fine-tuning was exotic and expensiveOpen-source fine-tuning is routine
Prompt engineering was a differentiatorPrompt patterns are public, shared, replicated

The model is now a utility — like electricity. Nobody wins because they have better electricity.

What "Context" Actually Means

Type of contextExamples
DocumentsKnowledge bases, product docs, SOPs, contracts
Structured dataCustomer history, transaction records, usage logs
Behavioral signalsWhat the user has clicked, skipped, edited, flagged
Temporal contextWhat's changed recently, what's expired, what's pending
Proprietary knowledgeResearch, methods, IP that only your company has

The model is the engine. Context is the fuel. The engine is the same for everyone. The fuel is where you win.

Why Context Creates a Compounding Advantage

  • Every interaction with your product produces new signal about the user, their patterns, their preferences
  • That signal feeds back into better context → better outputs → more user trust → more interactions
  • After 6 months of use, your product knows things about the user's workflow that a competitor starting fresh cannot replicate by switching models
  • The flywheel: use → signal → context → better output → more use

The longer a user stays, the worse a competitor's product looks by comparison — not because of the model, but because of accumulated context.

Module 2 · 7 Minutes

The Question That Reveals Your Moat

"If a competitor copied your model setup exactly — same model, same prompts, same architecture — would your product still be better?"

Yes: You have a context moat.    No: You are on borrowed ground.

For most AI products built in 2023–2024, the honest answer is No.

What a Context Moat Actually Looks Like

Pattern 1

Proprietary training signal

Your product improves because of interactions only your users have.

A legal AI that gets smarter from how lawyers at your firm edit its outputs — not public data.
Pattern 2

Embedded operational context

Your system knows the user's SOPs, org structure, history, language.

A sales AI that knows every deal your company has run for 5 years, not just generic training.
Pattern 3

Network-effect context

Context gets better as more people use the product.

A support AI that learns from how your best agents resolve tickets — the more tickets, the better the routing.

Warning Signs You're on Borrowed Ground

  • Your "AI feature" is a wrapper around an API with a good prompt
  • If someone read your system prompt, they could replicate your product in an afternoon
  • Your product doesn't get better the longer a user stays
  • Your data strategy is "we'll use what the model already knows"
  • You haven't defined what your product will know that competitors won't

None of these are fatal — if you catch them early. All of them are fatal if you ship a product on top of them and call it defensible.
